Power outage checklist

What to have ready before the lights go out, what to do in the first hours, and how to keep food, medicine and phones going until the power comes back.

Short answer

Before an outage, keep flashlights for everyone, a charged power bank, a battery or hand-crank radio, two weeks of water and food that needs no cooking, and a plan for any medicine that needs a fridge. When the power goes, keep the fridge and freezer shut (about 4 hours for the fridge, 48 for a full freezer), unplug electronics, use flashlights instead of candles, and never run a generator, grill or camp stove indoors.

A power cut changes more than the lights. The fridge warms up, phones run down, card terminals and gas pumps stop, and in winter or a heat wave the house itself becomes the problem. Most outages last a few hours; storms and grid failures can stretch them to days. Ready.gov and the American Red Cross both say the same thing: prepare before, and you will hardly notice the first day.

Before: what to have at home

  • A flashlight or headlamp for each person, and spare batteries
  • A charged power bank for every phone, and a car charger
  • A battery-powered or hand-crank radio, for news when the internet is down
  • Two weeks of water and non-perishable food, as the Red Cross recommends; the water and food guide has the amounts
  • A manual can opener
  • A cooler, and a fridge thermometer
  • Cash in small bills: ATMs and card readers stop with the power
  • A first aid kit and at least a week of prescription medicine
  • Warm blankets and layers for winter; a battery fan for summer
  • Carbon monoxide alarms with battery backup on every level of your home
  • Surge protectors for computers and TVs
  • A paper list of phone numbers, in case your phone dies

Also take stock of what in your home runs on electricity: a medical device, an electric garage door, a well pump, a phone with no battery backup. Find out how each one works without power, or what replaces it.

If someone relies on power for their health

Ready.gov's advice is to talk to your doctor before an outage happens: how long each refrigerated medicine keeps at room temperature, and what to do about any device that needs power, such as an oxygen concentrator or a CPAP. Keep spare batteries for it, and know where you would go if the outage lasts. Many communities open warming and cooling centers with charging stations; evacuate if a device cannot keep running.

During: the first hours

  1. Check whether it is just you. Look at the neighbors' lights and your circuit breaker, then report it to your utility and check its outage map from your phone.
  2. Unplug appliances and electronics. Power can come back with a surge. Leave one lamp switched on so you know when it returns.
  3. Keep the fridge and freezer closed. Eat fresh, perishable food first.
  4. Use flashlights, not candles. A candle knocked over or left burning in the dark is an easy way for an outage to become a fire.
  5. Save your phone. Lower the screen brightness, turn on low power mode, close apps you do not need and text instead of calling.
  6. Check on people near you, especially older neighbors, and tell family you are fine.

How long food lasts without power

Half-full freezerAbout 24 hoursFill the gaps with bags of ice or frozen water bottles

The figures are the Red Cross's and Ready.gov's. Check with a thermometer: food that has been above 40 °F (4 °C), especially meat, fish, dairy and eggs, should go. When in doubt, throw it out. Ask your pharmacist before using refrigerated medicine that has warmed up.

Heat, cooking and generators: stay safe

  • Generators, grills and camp stoves only outdoors, at least 20 feet (6 meters) from windows, doors and attached garages. Carbon monoxide has no smell and no color, and it kills.
  • Never heat your home with a gas stove or oven.
  • Plug appliances into the generator with heavy-duty extension cords, never into a wall outlet, and let it cool before refueling.
  • In extreme heat or cold, go to a cooling or warming center rather than sit it out.
  • Keep the car's tank at least half full, in case you need to leave or charge phones.
  • Stay at least 35 feet (10 meters) from fallen power lines and anything touching them, and call 911.

After the power comes back

Plug appliances back in one at a time. Go through the fridge and freezer with a thermometer and throw out what has warmed up. Restock what you used: the batteries, the water, the food, the power bank. Then write down what you were missing, because that is the most useful list you will ever make.

Frequently asked questions

How long does food last in the fridge without power?

About 4 hours if you keep the door shut. A full freezer keeps its temperature for about 48 hours, a half-full one for about 24. Throw out perishable food that has been above 40 °F (4 °C).

What should I buy before a power outage?

Flashlights and batteries, a power bank for each phone, a battery or hand-crank radio, two weeks of water and food that needs no cooking, a manual can opener, a cooler and carbon monoxide alarms with battery backup.

Is it safe to use a candle during a power outage?

Flashlights are safer. The Red Cross advises flashlights rather than candles: a candle knocked over or left burning in the dark can start a fire.

Can I run a generator in the garage?

No. Generators must run outdoors, at least 20 feet (6 meters) from windows, doors and attached garages, because of carbon monoxide, even with the garage door open.

How do I keep my phone charged in a long outage?

Keep a charged power bank for each phone, use low power mode and lower the screen brightness, text rather than call, and charge in the car if you have to, with the engine running only outdoors.
