Pet emergency kit

A go bag for your dog or cat: what to pack, how to plan an evacuation with pets, and how to get them into the carrier when every minute counts.

Short answer

A pet emergency kit holds several days of food and water in waterproof containers, a bowl, a week of any medicine, a leash or harness with a spare ID tag, a carrier or crate, copies of vaccination and microchip records, litter or waste bags, a familiar toy or blanket, and a photo of you with your pet. Plan where your pet can stay, since many shelters and hotels do not accept animals, and if you are told to evacuate, take your pets with you.

Pets are family, and in an emergency they depend on you completely. Ready.gov is direct about it: if local officials ask you to evacuate, your pet should evacuate too, because pets left behind can end up lost, injured or worse. The hard part is rarely the packing. It is finding somewhere that takes animals, and catching a frightened cat in the five minutes you have.

The pet kit checklist

Ready.gov suggests two kits: a larger one for sheltering at home, and a light one to grab when you leave. This is the light one.

  • Food for several days, in an airtight, waterproof container
  • Water for several days, and a collapsible bowl
  • An extra supply of any medicine your pet takes, in a waterproof container
  • A pet first aid kit: ask your vet what yours should hold
  • A collar with an ID tag, a harness and leash, and a spare of each
  • A carrier or crate for each pet, labeled with your name and phone number
  • Copies of vaccination records, registration and microchip number, in a waterproof pouch and on your phone
  • Litter and a disposable tray for cats; waste bags for dogs
  • Paper towels, garbage bags and a little pet shampoo for cleaning up
  • A favorite toy, treats or bedding, which helps with stress
  • A recent photo of you together with your pet, to prove ownership if you are separated
  • Your vet's name and number, and feeding and medicine instructions in case someone else has to care for your pet

Make a plan for your pet

  • Know where you can go. Many public shelters and hotels do not allow pets inside. Make a list now of pet-friendly hotels, boarding kennels and friends or relatives along your evacuation routes.
  • Set up a buddy system. Agree with a neighbor or friend that whoever is home will collect the other's pets if needed, and give them a key.
  • Microchip your pet, and keep the registration up to date with your phone number and an out-of-area contact.
  • Leave early. Evacuating with animals takes longer, and waiting for the order can mean leaving without them.
  • Bring pets indoors at the first warning of a storm, so you are not searching for them when it is time to go.

The pet plan belongs in your family emergency plan, so everyone knows who takes which animal.

Get them used to the carrier now

A cat that has only seen its carrier on the way to the vet will hide the moment it appears. Ready.gov's tips are simple and they work:

  • Leave the carrier out, open, in a room your pet uses
  • Feed treats next to it, then inside it
  • Put a familiar blanket or small bed inside so it smells like home
  • Note where your pets hide, so you can find them quickly

Keep the kit fresh

Pet food and medicine expire like yours do. Check the kit when you check your own every six months: rotate the food into everyday meals before its date, refresh the water, update the photo and the vaccination records, and make sure the harness still fits a growing puppy.

Frequently asked questions

What goes in a pet emergency kit?

Several days of food and water, a bowl, extra medicine, a pet first aid kit, a collar with ID tag and a spare leash, a carrier, copies of vaccination and microchip records, litter or waste bags, a familiar toy and a photo of you with your pet.

Can I bring my pet to an evacuation shelter?

Not always: many public shelters and hotels do not allow pets inside, though service animals are accepted. Find pet-friendly hotels, kennels or friends along your route before you need them, and ask your local emergency office how pets are handled.

How much food and water should I pack for my pet?

Ready.gov suggests several days of food and water in the grab-and-go kit, and a larger supply at home for sheltering in place. Pack what your pet normally eats, so a stressful day does not also bring a change of diet.

Should I leave my pet at home during an evacuation?

No. If it is not safe for you to stay, it is not safe for your pet. Pets left behind can be lost or injured, so take them with you, and leave early if you can.
